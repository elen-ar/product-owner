# Creating a Single Intake and Prioritization Process for Three Corporate Websites

## Challenge

When I joined the company, there was no dedicated owner for the corporate websites.

Requests for changes, fixes, SEO improvements, content updates, and new functionality were sent directly to developers by various stakeholders across the organization. Each request followed its own path, requirements were often incomplete, and communication happened across multiple channels.

As a result:

* Developers spent significant time gathering missing information instead of building solutions.
* Priorities were unclear and frequently changed.
* Request status was difficult to track.
* Stakeholders had no clear understanding of where to submit requests or how decisions were made.
* There was no consistent process for managing work across three corporate websites.

The company needed a scalable way to handle website requests while reducing friction between business teams and development.

## My Role

As the person responsible for website operations and development coordination, I took ownership of the intake and prioritization process.

Working closely with a Jira administrator and the development team, I designed and implemented a new workflow for managing all website-related requests.

## Approach

### Establishing a Single Point of Entry

I introduced a centralized intake process for all website requests.

Instead of contacting developers directly, stakeholders now submit requests through a dedicated workflow managed by my team.

This created a single source of truth for all website-related work.

### Structuring Incoming Requests

To improve prioritization and routing, I introduced request categorization based on:

* Website affected
* Request type
* Business impact

Requests are now classified into categories such as:

* Content corrections
* Bug fixes
* SEO and analytics improvements
* Content changes
* Functional enhancements
* New website initiatives

This allows the team to quickly identify the appropriate review and delivery path.

### Introducing a Triage Process

Before a request reaches development, my team performs an initial assessment.

We:

* Clarify business goals
* Gather missing requirements
* Consult with developers when necessary
* Evaluate alternative solutions
* Prioritize requests based on value and effort

In some cases, we recommend a simpler or more cost-effective solution instead of the originally requested implementation.

### Connecting Business and Development Workflows

One of the key improvements was creating a structured escalation process between marketing and development teams.

When engineering work is required, a linked development task can be created directly from the business request while preserving all context, discussion history, and requirements.

After implementation is completed, ownership automatically returns to the business side for validation, stakeholder communication, and final closure.

This established a transparent end-to-end workflow across both teams.

## Results

* Created a single intake process for all website-related requests across three corporate websites.
* Established clear ownership of request evaluation and prioritization.
* Centralized communication and decision-making within Jira.
* Reduced the need for developers to gather requirements directly from stakeholders.
* Improved visibility of request status and progress.
* Implemented a structured handoff process between business and development teams.
* Enabled more consistent prioritization and resource allocation across multiple websites.

## Key Takeaway

The core problem was not the absence of a ticketing system.

It was the absence of ownership and a structured decision-making process.

By introducing a centralized intake, triage, and prioritization workflow, I transformed a fragmented request management model into a scalable process that allows business teams and developers to work more efficiently while maintaining transparency and accountability.
