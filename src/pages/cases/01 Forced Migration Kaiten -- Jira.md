**Forced Migration, Zero Losses: How I Moved All Team Processes to a New Task System Without Losing a Thing**

Managing a large multilingual website means living with constant parallel workstreams — ongoing support, new projects, multiple stakeholders, overlapping deadlines. Losing task history, context, or team alignment during a tool switch isn't just inconvenient. It can cost weeks. When I became Head of Web Services, we had exactly that challenge ahead of us: an urgent, non-negotiable migration from Kaiten to Jira.

**Emergency Migration, Zero Data Loss**

Once the Jira project structure was set up, roles assigned, and workflows established, we imported the full task archive — all 17,000 tickets. Every historical case remained searchable. I wrote a detailed guide for internal stakeholders on how to submit tasks, notified everyone in advance, and the backlog started filling up within days of launch. This entire transition happened while I was personally relocating to another country. It finished on schedule regardless.

**New Discipline: Jira or Nothing**

From day one, all task-related communication happened exclusively in Jira — no exceptions. This eliminated data loss and ensured full transparency: any team member could immediately understand a task's context, status, and any issues that had come up — even if the original owner was sick or on vacation. Weekly syncs kept everyone aligned on priorities across overlapping workstreams from multiple internal clients.

**Jira in the Long Run**

Jira initially felt as bloated as YouTrack. It proved its real value later — during a major corporate website migration to a new DXP platform, where its powerful search and per-project board views let us track strategic milestones and day-to-day workload simultaneously. A tool we were forced to adopt became one we'd choose again.
